Spoken output helps presence and accessibility when it is the spoken line (with emotion) as a complementary channel; speaking the printed log and asides as well is the redundancy-plus-commentary condition that splits attention—so dialogue-only this cycle, not TTS-full and not skip.

1. **Concurrent identical or summarized on-screen text with narration plus animation hurts retention and transfer.** Mayer, Heiser, and Lonn (2001) (https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.93.1.187). Adding text that duplicated or summarized the spoken explanation overloaded the visual channel and split attention between animation and print; interesting but irrelevant spoken “seductive details” also cut transfer. Implication: TTS-full (log + SpeakerCue.text + asides, spoken while the terminal still prints) is this hurt condition.

2. **Spoken-plus-printed words help only when there is no concurrent visual stream.** Moreno and Mayer (2002) (https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.94.1.156). Dual presentation beat speech-only provided no animation ran at the same time. Implication: the RPG already has concurrent visuals (sim, log, asides). Dual-coding is not a license to speak every printed fragment.

3. **Even without diagrams, simultaneous identical spoken and written technical text is less efficient than speech alone.** Kalyuga, Chandler, and Sweller (2004) (https://doi.org/10.1518/hfes.46.3.567.50405). Coordinating two verbal streams generated extraneous load; sequential or single-mode beat concurrent. Implication: “read and hear the same words” is not a free win for players who can already read.

4. **Across 57 studies, spoken-written did not beat written-only overall; any dual advantage over speech-only was limited to low prior knowledge, system-paced, picture-free materials, and key-term labels beat verbatim dual text.** Adesope and Nesbit (2012) (https://doi.org/10.1037/a0026147). Implication: a self-paced printed log gets little comprehension gain from being spoken.

5. **Verbatim on-screen text with narration impairs learning; a small discrepancy helps; too much discrepancy is as bad as identical; learners still prefer the identical pairing.** Yue, Bjork, and Bjork (2013) (https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031971). Implication: preference for read-along is a metacognitive trap (over-reliance). Visual asides that are not clones of the line are the slight-discrepancy case; speaking asides is extra commentary.

6. **Long spoken streams reverse the modality benefit because speech is transient.** Leahy and Sweller (2011) (https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.1787). Visual text plus diagram beat long audio plus diagram; shortening the audio reinstated the modality effect. Implication: TTS-full of log + asides + line is a long transient dump; keep the spoken channel short (SpeakerCue.text only).

7. **Reading-while-listening vs reading-only: trivial overall benefit (g = .18), and only when experimenter-paced (g = .41); self-paced g = .06, not reliable.** Clinton-Lisell (2023), *Educational Research: Theory and Practice*, 34(3), 133–155 (https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1403866). Implication: the terminal is self-paced; hearing the same words does not improve comprehension for typical readers.

8. **Voice is a social-presence channel, but quality, emotion, identity, and mix all fail independently of the words.** Nass and Lee (2001) (https://doi.org/10.1037/1076-898X.7.3.171) found people attribute personality to synthetic speech and prefer voice–content consistency. Ferro Filho et al. (2026) (https://doi.org/10.1109/AIxVR67263.2026.00042) found NPC-TTS clarity is solved (>4.5) while naturalness, emotional adequacy, and speaker identity still differentiate; Chatterbox led, unstable identity broke the illusion. Xbox XAG 105 (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/105) requires separate dialogue vs music/SFX sliders and auto-ducking when a screen reader speaks, because game audio collides with both NPC speech and AT. Implication: ship dialogue-only with emotion on SpeakerCue; keep ducking; keep game-TTS off the screen-reader bus.

If believed, lock dialogue-only this cycle: TTS SpeakerCue.text (emotion/speed as manner), asides stay visual, terminal still prints, ducking stays. Forbid TTS-full (log and asides spoken), a second narrator over the line, and voice without a mute/separate dialogue slider and screen-reader duck. Skip only if local TTS cannot hold speaker identity and emotion; Chatterbox already scored best on those axes.
