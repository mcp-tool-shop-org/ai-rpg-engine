Presence in a tick-authoritative RPG is a **per-turn composed beat plus continuous ambient world-state**, with high-salience packages reserved for **key moments**; per-event juice is the documented failure mode (habituation, agency occlusion, inattentional deafness).

1. **None and Extreme juiciness both cut play time, player experience, motivation, and in-game performance versus Medium/High.** Kao 2020 (*The effects of juiciness in an action RPG*, DOI 10.1016/j.entcom.2020.100359; N=3,018). Implication: fanfare-every-kill is Extreme; budget Medium/High juice at the turn, not every simulation event.

2. **Amplifying feedback on every action reduced effectance and competence (it occluded action–outcome binding); success-dependence raised all motives; curiosity was the only playtime predictor.** Kao, Ballou, Gerling, Breitsohl & Deterding 2024 (*How does Juicy Game Feedback Motivate?*, DOI 10.1145/3613904.3642656; n=1,699, pre-registered). Implication: juice graded success (hit vs crit vs clear), not every tick event; “too much of a good thing” when amplification hides what mattered.

3. **Juicing exists to communicate event importance; over-juicing makes mechanical importance unreadable. Turn-based titles already use a composed beat: plan without time pressure, then a single intense payoff (XCOM, Fallout V.A.T.S., Bejeweled).** Pichlmair & Johansen 2021 (*Designing Game Feel: A Survey*, arXiv:2011.09201, DOI 10.1109/TG.2021.3072241). Swink’s “real-time control” definition (2009) excludes this clock; transferring per-event action-game juice is a category error. Implication: one AudioDirector beat per tick, not a cue per emitted event.

4. **Developers locate “world felt” in ambient state available without input, and in exaggeration that details state change; feedback must reflect event importance (coherence).** Hicks, Dickinson, Holopainen & Gerling 2018 (*Good Game Feel*, DOI 10.26503/dl.v2018i1.936; 17 developers). B4: ambient without explicit input makes the world “real and interactive”; A4: “Does feedback reflect the importance of the event?” Implication: AmbientCue[] as idle presence; UiEffect/SfxCue exaggeration only on state change.

5. **Visual embellishments raise aesthetic appeal in every tested game, but competence, curiosity, and presence only in some.** Hicks, Gerling, Dickinson & Vanden Abeele 2019 (*Juicy Game Design*, DOI 10.1145/3311350.3347171; n=40 then n=32). Implication: do not treat per-event flash/shake/particles as presence; they are appeal, and often inert.

6. **Workload drops at large subtask boundaries; interrupting there cut resumption lag 69% and annoyance 18% versus high-workload moments (N=12 follow-up). Random mid-task interrupts cost up to 30% more time and 2× errors.** Bailey & Iqbal 2008 (*Understanding changes in mental workload…*, DOI 10.1145/1314683.1314689). Implication: fire the composed beat at the turn/tick boundary, not mid-resolution per combat event.

7. **Under high visual load, 39% of pilots missed a critical auditory alarm they 100% heard when unoccupied.** Dehais, Causse, Vachon, Régis, Menant & Tremblay 2014 (*Failure to Detect Critical Auditory Alerts in the Cockpit*, DOI 10.1177/0018720813510735, PMID 25029890; n=28). Implication: dense SfxCue[] during combat resolution will not be heard; reserve high-salience audio for post-resolution key moments.

8. **~90% of operators probability-match response rate to alarm reliability (cry-wolf); they stop treating frequent cues as true.** Bliss, Gilson & Deaton 1995 (*Human probability matching behaviour in response to alarms of varying reliability*, DOI 10.1080/00140139508925269; n=138). Implication: nine listeners fanfaring every kill trains ignore. Same pattern as text+audio: Jennes, Blanckaert & Van den Broeck 2023 (DOI 10.1145/3573381.3596151) found an “immersion paradox”—soundtrack can deepen or yank the reader out of a digital story.

If believed, the plan changes from event-fanned presentation to: (a) AmbientCue[] as continuous world-state between inputs; (b) one composed AudioCommand[] beat per tick at the turn boundary; (c) a short allow-list of key-moment packages (zone entry, encounter.cleared, dialogue beat) with reserved coherence. It forbids per-event fanfare, Extreme juiciness, and high-salience cues mid-resolution.
